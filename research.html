Dissertation

Truthmakers, Quantification, and Ontological Commitment

In my dissertation, I develop and defend a truthmaker-based criterion of ontological commitment and a truthmaker theory that makes use of sui generis higher-order quantification. For the first purpose, I motivate the search for a new criterion of ontological commitment by criticizing the orthodox criterion, namely the Quinean criterion. I then argue that a truthmaker-based criterion, especially the version I develop, is preferable both to the Quinean criterion and to rival truthmaker-based criteria. For the second purpose, I argue that sui generis higher-order quantification helps truthmaker theorists preserve the idea that truths depend on reality without incurring unattractive ontological commitments. I focus in particular on negative truths, such as the truth that there are no unicorns. Such truths pose a familiar problem for truthmaker theory: if a truth must depend on reality, what in reality accounts for the truth that something does not exist? I argue that understanding properties as higher-order beings provides a promising answer to this problem while avoiding difficulties faced by theories that appeal to first-order entities, such as universals or tropes, as truthmakers for negative truths.

Published Articles

Against Modal Humeanism

· Analytic Philosophy, Forthcoming

Ted Sider defends mereological nihilism against the possibility of gunk. He argues that if we accept modal Humeanism, then the possibility of gunk poses no threat to nihilism. This paper argues that Sider's argument fails because nihilism remains vulnerable to the possibility of gunk, even under modal Humeanism. Section 2 explains modal Humeanism in detail. Section 3 presents Sider's argument that nihilism is unaffected by the possibility of gunk. Section 4 provides a counterargument. Section 5 anticipates potential objections and responds to them.

Dissertation

Truthmakers, Quantification, and Ontological Commitment

In my dissertation, I develop and defend two connected proposals: a truthmaker-based criterion of ontological commitment and a truthmaker theory that uses sui generis higher-order quantification. Truthmaker theory holds that truths depend on things in reality that make them true, and a criterion of ontological commitment tells us which entities a theory must assume to exist for its claims to be true. In developing the first proposal, I criticize the orthodox Quinean criterion, which reads a theory's commitments off the entities its quantifiers range over. I argue that this ties commitment to the wrong thing, and that we should instead look to what must exist to make a theory's claims true. I then defend my own version of such a criterion and argue that it is preferable not only to the Quinean criterion but also to rival truthmaker-based criteria. Whether such a criterion is worth having depends on whether the underlying truthmaker theory can be made to work. The hardest case is negative truths, such as the truth that there are no unicorns: if every truth depends on reality, what in reality accounts for the truth that something does not exist? I argue that sui generis higher-order quantification, which generalizes into non-nominal positions and is not reducible to ordinary quantification over objects, lets truthmaker theorists answer this question without incurring unattractive ontological commitments. Treating properties as higher-order beings, rather than as first-order entities such as universals or tropes, yields a promising account of truthmakers for negative truths.

Published Articles

Against Modal Humeanism

· Analytic Philosophy, Forthcoming

Ted Sider defends mereological nihilism against the possibility of gunk. He argues that if we accept modal Humeanism, then the possibility of gunk poses no threat to nihilism. This paper argues that Sider's argument fails because nihilism remains vulnerable to the possibility of gunk, even under modal Humeanism. Section 2 explains modal Humeanism in detail. Section 3 presents Sider's argument that nihilism is unaffected by the possibility of gunk. Section 4 provides a counterargument. Section 5 anticipates potential objections and responds to them.
